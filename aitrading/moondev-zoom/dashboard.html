Then work the loop in README.md: capture → watch → notes from memory → rebuild by typing → review. Budget about twice the stream length, and do fewer sessions properly rather than all of them passively.

Standing orders

Rules

The full versions live in ROADMAP.md. The don'ts matter more than the dos right now: getting a do wrong makes you slower, getting a don't wrong costs money.

Do

  • Rebuild by typing. Cloning teaches you where his files are. Typing teaches you what they do.
  • Take one idea per session. A four-hour stream has forty. Carry one. You have 81 bots already — ideas are not the bottleneck.
  • Write notes from memory first. Then correct against the transcript. The corrections are the map of what you missed.
  • Timebox. Two hours of rebuilding beats eight hours of watching, and it isn't close.
  • Keep his tree and your tree separate. Reference code in one place, validated code in another.
  • Record the negative results. "Degraded out-of-sample, dropped" is a real finding you will otherwise re-derive in four months.
  • Read the repos, not just the streams. The stream is a performance of the code; the repo is the code.
  • Treat the stream as the start of the work. Not the end of it.

Don't

  • Don't trade real money on a strategy you saw on a livestream. Not small, not "just to feel it". A demoed strategy has an in-sample result and no out-of-sample result — which means it has no result.
  • Don't treat a demo as evidence. He found the strategy, then showed it working on the data he found it in. That's in-sample by construction. Not dishonest — structural.
  • Don't confuse "it ran" with "it works." A bot that executes cleanly and loses steadily is a working program and a broken strategy.
  • Don't let AI-written backtests through unread. Lookahead bias is the failure mode: indicators computed before the split, a signal peeking at its own bar, a fill at a price not available at decision time. They make beautiful equity curves.
  • Don't skip the backtest because the idea is obviously good. Obviously-good is where overfitting lives.
  • Don't commit secrets. No .env, no keys in notebooks, screenshots or pasted tracebacks. Read-only keys where they'll do.
  • Don't run anyone's script against a funded account. His, a viewer's, or one an AI wrote for you. Read it fully, run it on paper.
  • Don't chase every new repo. He ships constantly. Following all of it feels busy and teaches nothing.
  • Don't measure progress in hours watched. Easiest number to move, least connected to whether you can do anything.
  • Don't buy your way past the work. Nothing purchased is exempt from your own out-of-sample test.
  • Don't take only AI and livestreams as feedback. Both will happily agree with you about a bad strategy. Your walk-forward module won't.
