The finding

Every bot was ranked twice: once on the price history it was chosen on, and once on a hidden last-twelve-months slice it had never seen. The two rankings barely agree.

Of the top 20 bots on visible history, four were still profitable on hidden history.

trend_follow_bot ranked 3rd with +865% and finished 76th at −30%. cmf_bot ranked 4th with +2,383% and scraped 11th at +12%. Meanwhile mfi_bot ranked 57th, having lost 99.7%, and came 3rd at +57%. A backtest leaderboard built on a single history is close to noise.

The survivors are real but currently unusable.

The median worst drawdown among the fifteen survivors is −46%. The best-ranked bot, roc_bot, returned +106% while dropping 60% from its peak along the way. A risk overlay already exists in the codebase — volatility sizing, trailing stop, drawdown circuit breaker — and has not been wired into the scan. That is the highest-value hour of work currently available.

Progress

How far along the pipeline we are

Nothing goes near real money until it clears six checks in order. Each check is built to kill ideas that only looked good by luck. Two are cleared.

01

Historical backtest

done

Replay all 136 bots over roughly six years of SOL prices with realistic fees and slippage, then rank them by risk-adjusted return.

02

Out-of-sample test

done

Hide the most recent stretch of history, choose bots using only the older part, then score them on the part they were never chosen on. Five cut points, one command, about 25 seconds.

03

Walk-forward test

next

Slide the window forward repeatedly — choose on one period, trade the next untouched one, repeat. This is what shows whether an edge survives changing conditions rather than one lucky slice. It is also the harness the automated researcher plugs into later.

04

Paper trading

later

Run the survivors against live prices with fake money and compare what actually happens to what the backtest predicted.

05

Live monitoring

later

Watch the paper strategy for weeks or months, looking for decay, before trusting it any further.

06

Real execution

not until greenlit

Its own separate decision, taken deliberately. Nothing in the code today can place a real order.

Ideas

What’s next

Eight directions worth considering, argued from what the data actually showed rather than from what would be fun to build. The first three are the ones I would take.

One · free

Stop adding bots. Start killing them.

You have 136 bots and evidence for roughly three. The 137th bot is worth much less than proving one of the fifteen survivors is real, because the bottleneck is no longer the supply of ideas — it is the cost of testing one properly. Freeze the library at 136 and spend the next stretch entirely on validation.

The tellAdding seasonality_bot cost an hour and it lost 73% out-of-sample across 157 trades. That is a very expensive hour.

Two · small

Rank on drawdown, not on return.

Every survivor is unusable as it stands, at a median −46% worst drawdown. Sortino penalises downside volatility but it does not penalise the single deep hole that actually makes a strategy impossible to sit through. Re-rank the field on MAR — return divided by max drawdown — or an Ulcer index, and see whether the shortlist changes. A bot that returned +30% at −16% drawdown is worth more to you than one that returned +106% at −60%.

Already visiblevol_trade_bot ranks 12th on return but has the shallowest drawdown in the top group at −16%. rsi_revert_bot is 8th at −22%. On a drawdown-aware ranking both move up sharply.

Three · medium

Test the basket, not the members.

The survivors trade on genuinely different signals — rate of change, volume force, trend strength, an Ichimoku cloud — and their exposures differ wildly, from 4% of bars to 100%. Strategies that are individually mediocre and mutually uncorrelated combine into something with a much shallower drawdown than any member. Backtest an equal-weight basket of the top four as though it were a single bot and compare its drawdown to theirs.

Why it may workA basket is the one intervention that attacks the drawdown problem without requiring any new edge to exist.

Four · medium

Multi-symbol before multi-strategy.

Eighty-one bots on one price series gives you a single draw from the universe of possible market histories. Six bots across fifteen symbols gives you ninety. The second is far more informative per hour spent, and it is the cheapest way to find out whether these edges are properties of markets or properties of SOL between 2020 and 2026.

Watch forIf a bot only works on SOL that is not automatically fatal — but you need to know it, and right now you cannot.

Five · medium

Build the gauntlet, then never hand-run a test again.

You now have most of the pieces of a fixed qualification path: holdout scan, walk-forward, a second symbol, a trade-count floor. Wire them into one command that takes a bot name and returns pass or fail with the reason. After that, evaluating a new idea costs one line instead of a session, and the standard cannot drift when you are tired or excited about a result.

This is the actual productThe stated goal was an automated strategy researcher. The gauntlet is that researcher — the bots are only its input.

Six · small

Give the researcher a scoreboard it writes to.

Right now the record of what has been tried lives in session summaries and regenerated result folders. Have every run append one row to a single ledger — date, bot, config, window, result, commit SHA. Progress then accumulates in a file you can query instead of in memory you have to reconstruct, and this dashboard can read straight from it.

CompoundingIt is the difference between a project that remembers what it learned and one that re-derives it every week.

Seven · medium

Use the Uniswap sniper as the Stage 4 paper venue.

Stage 4 needs somewhere to run survivors forward against live prices with fake money and hard limits. The v4 sniper already has price and liquidity reads, a position cap, a loss cap, a slippage cap and a kill switch. That is most of a paper-trading rig, built for a different purpose. Point it at the shortlist rather than building a second one.

CaveatDifferent venue and different microstructure to the CSV backtests, so the reconciliation step matters more here, not less.

Eight · small

Run the scan nightly and read only the diff.

The holdout scan takes 25 seconds. Run it on a schedule against fresh data and diff the shortlist against yesterday’s. Strategy decay then shows up as a line changing rather than as something you happen to re-check in three weeks. It also gives you an honest answer to “is this still working” without re-reading anything.

Cheapest item hereA cron entry, a diff and one notification.
